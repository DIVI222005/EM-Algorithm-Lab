MODULE 01

The Mystery of Hidden Data

Imagine three types of customers mixed in one dataset, but nobody wrote down which type each customer is. The group label is a latent (hidden) variable. We only see the points, and we want to find the groups and describe each one.

Real life never gives you this button.
MODULES 02 + 03

Gaussian Mixtures & the E-step

A Gaussian Mixture Model says each point came from one of K bell curves, each with a mean μ, a spread σ² and a mixing weight π (how often it is chosen). The E-step asks: given the current curves, how responsible is each curve for a point x? Move the white probe (or click the graph) and watch the probabilities change.

MODULE 04

The M-step: Maximization

Now pretend the responsibilities are right and refit each curve. Every point votes for each cluster in proportion to its responsibility, so the new mean is a weighted average, the new variance a weighted spread, and the new weight the cluster’s share of total responsibility. Curves slide toward the points they own, and then the E-step changes its mind again. Repeat until nothing moves.

E Guess hidden labels softly, using the current parameters.
M Re-estimate parameters, using those soft labels.
↻ Each loop never lowers the likelihood.

MODULE 06

The Mathematics

Model: p(x) = Σₖ πₖ 𝒩(x | μₖ, σ²ₖ). Latent label zᵢ ∈ {1…K}. Maximizing log L directly is hard (a log of a sum), so EM alternates two easy steps:

E: γᵢₖ = πₖ 𝒩(xᵢ|μₖ,σ²ₖ) / Σⱼ πⱼ 𝒩(xᵢ|μⱼ,σ²ⱼ)
M: Nₖ = Σᵢ γᵢₖ   μₖ = Σᵢ γᵢₖxᵢ / Nₖ   σ²ₖ = Σᵢ γᵢₖ(xᵢ−μₖ)² / Nₖ   πₖ = Nₖ / n
👆 Hover or tap any pink dotted symbol in the equations to see what it means.
log L = Σᵢ log Σₖ πₖ 𝒩(xᵢ|μₖ,σ²ₖ)

In 2D isotropic form the variance update divides by 2Nₖ. Each EM round raises a lower bound on log L that touches it at the current parameters, so log L never decreases (it can stop at a local optimum).

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

Finding two hidden groups in height data

A health survey measured the heights of 240 adults but never recorded sex. The histogram looks like one lumpy hill. EM figures out that it is really two overlapping groups (for example women and men), without ever seeing a label. The data is simulated to resemble real adult height distributions.

Start: a poor guess (means 155 and 185 cm, wide spread). Each iteration, the E-step splits every bar between the two groups (cyan and magenta share), and the M-step moves each curve to fit the share it owns. Watch the curves slide into place.

Customer segmentation in 3D

A shop has 180 customers described by age, income and spending score, but no segment labels. EM fits three 3D Gaussian clouds and discovers the groups.
